# Results (Oct 22, 2026)

Every number below is regenerated by the scripts named next to it. The PUM side is model-only (the integrator, VIPER's closed form generalized), with technology numbers from `lib/tech` (several marked VERIFY). The GPU side is measured on this host's RTX 3080.

## Workload (T, `figs/fig_trace_profile.py`)

- **100 SWE-bench Verified sessions**, run with mini-swe-agent 1.17.5 and mini-coder-4b (Q8_0) on the 3080, on a repo-stratified sample. 97 submitted a patch. The 89 sessions collected first are the frozen search/held-out workload (`traces/workload_main.tsv`); the last 11 are the second held-out set.
- Time on the 3080 splits into **decode 91%, prefill 4%, tools 5%** (all 100 sessions). On the frozen 89 the split is 90/4/6. Tool time excludes the ~0.28 s container exec overhead per call; raw times are in `results/traces/swebench_summary.tsv`.
- Final context: median 15.5K tokens, max 64K. **11% of sessions (11/100) ran out of VRAM with fp16 KV** (7/89 = 8% of the frozen workload, 4/11 = 36% of the late set): the wall is at 32–36K, and the trace server ran with a 32K context. They were re-run with int8 KV at 64K. One of them (xarray-4094, 218 turns) outgrew even 64K.
- Cross-check (G3): replaying each trace through the llama-bench decode curve predicts the measured decode time within −2 to −3%.

## Headline design (`experiments/search_compare.py --tag main`)

The Domeline walk finds it in 28 evaluations (45 search sessions):

- Crossbars (ISAAC geometry, transposable K writes) for all weight MVMs, the LM head and attention; 64 fixed-function nonlinear units for softmax, norms, RoPE and SiLU; embedding and sampling on the CPU; CXL-class link; int8 KV. 2.16M arrays.
- **9.42× faster than the RTX 3080 on the search sessions, 9.61× on the 44 held-out sessions.** The Amdahl bound (tools only) is 16.7×.
- Decode is 0.40 ms/token at 4K context and 0.77 ms/token at 64K (3080: 7.5 ms empty, fp16 KV, to 27 ms at 64K, int8 KV). The crossbar read cycle (348 µs/token) is the floor. At long context, score traffic between the crossbars and the nonlinear unit is the next term (transfer: 28% of LLM time).
- Area: **9,729 mm² at ISAAC's 32 nm tile density, ~15× the 3080's die.** Effort: 7 months.

## The walk (`figs/fig_walk.py main`)

0.04× (crossbar MVMs, everything else on the host) → attention to crossbars 0.45× → softmax to nonlinear unit 0.80× → LM head to crossbars 1.39× → norms off the host 1.75× → unit sizing 2.5–6.5× → transposed K writes 7.4× → norms to nonlinear unit 8.5× → 9.42×. Then shrink: 18.8K → 9.7K mm² at the same speed. Every move cites the reading that justified it.

## Search efficiency (`figs/fig_search.py`)

Budget 100 evaluations on the search set; "best" = the smallest design within 1% of the fastest found.

| | Unconstrained | Effort ≤ 5 months |
|---|---|---|
| Domeline walk | 9.42× at eval 28 | 8.00× at eval 33 |
| TPE (3 seeds) | 9.46–9.48× at 77–99 | 3.27 / 7.02 / 8.05× |
| Hill climbing, same moves, time only (3 seeds) | 9.47 / 9.47 / 3.26× | 3.08 / 3.26 / 8.01× |
| Random (3 seeds) | 3.1–8.2× | 0.84–3.06× |
| Sonnet agent + readings | 9.47× at eval 14 | 8.00–8.01× at 11–13 |
| Sonnet agent, time only | 9.47× at eval 6 | 8.00–8.05× at 12–15 |
| Haiku agent + readings (3 seeds) | — | 8.05 / 8.05 / 7.02× |
| Haiku agent, time only (3 seeds) | — | 8.05 / 8.05 / 8.00× |

What this supports:
1. The readings make algorithmic search reliable: the walk always finds the best design in about 30 evaluations; hill climbing over the same single-knob moves, without readings, finds it on 1–2 of 3 seeds.
2. Classical searchers need 3× more evaluations (TPE) or fail (random).
3. **LLM agents do not need the readings in this space.** Their hardware priors, plus the knob descriptions, find the optimum in 6–15 evaluations. Don't claim the readings help LLM agents.
4. At equal speed the walk's design costs less effort (7 vs 10–16 months), because the shrink phase drops the on-die link that only buys the last 0.5%.

## Effort staircase (`experiments/effort_sweep.py`, `figs/fig_effort.py`)

0 months 0.59× (crossbars only) → 1 month 3.07× (vector unit) → 4 months 8.00× (nonlinear unit) → 7 months 9.42× (transposed K writes). TPE finds nothing feasible below 4 months in 150 evaluations.

## Sensitivity (`experiments/sensitivity.py`, `figs/fig_sensitivity.py`)

Base 9.52× on all 89 sessions. Each uncertain number is scaled by ½ and 2, with the walk re-optimizing. Only the **crossbar read cycle** moves the headline much (11.0× / 7.5×). Nonlinear-unit throughput and bandwidth move it by ≤4%. Link, crossbar write latency, vector-unit throughput and the host attention kernel move it by <1%. **Per-array crossbar area moves area linearly (4.9K–19.5K mm²) and leaves speed unchanged.** The VERIFY entries that matter are the read cycle and the per-array area.

## Exhaustive sweep (`experiments/sweep_all.py`, `sweep_analyze.py`, `figs/fig_sweep.py`)

All 575,400 distinct designs on the 45 search sessions (11.5 h on 20 cores). 280,296 are feasible.

- **The optimum under the model is 9.48×.** The walk's design (9.42×, 9,729 mm², 7 months) is the smallest design within 1% of it, which is the rule every searcher's "best" uses.
- **Good designs are rare.** Only 3.8% of feasible designs beat the 3080. 96 designs (0.03%) are within 1% of the optimum, and 144 are within 10%. Random search's failure follows directly from this.
- **The effort staircase is exact.** The best design under each effort cap in the sweep matches what the walk found: 0.59 / 3.08 / 8.00 / 9.42×.
- **How close each searcher got, unconstrained (gap to 9.48×, eval of first design within 1%):**
  - walk: 0.7% (the smallest-design rule), at eval 20;
  - TPE: 0.07–0.29%, at evals 41–69;
  - hill climbing: 0.17% on 2 of 3 seeds, at evals 56–78; 66% on the third;
  - random: 14–68%, never;
  - Sonnet agent: 0.18%, at eval 10 with readings and eval 4 without.
- **Under the 5-month cap (optimum 8.05×):**
  - walk: 0.56%, at eval 24;
  - TPE: within 1% on 1 of 3 seeds;
  - hill climbing: within 1% on 1 of 3 seeds;
  - Sonnet and Haiku agents: within 1% in 9–15 evals on 9 of 10 runs.
- Exhaustive search needs 575,400 evaluations to find the optimum. The walk needs 20–30, so this space did not need brute force. The model makes brute force affordable here, but not with a gem5-class evaluator at 1–3 h per point.

## Second held-out set (`experiments/heldout_extra.py`)

These are 11 SWE-bench tasks that Docker Hub rate limits blocked during collection. They were run after every search had finished, so no search, library or space decision saw them. The design is built exactly as in the search.

- 4 of 11 hit the fp16 wall (36%, vs 7/89 = 8% in the frozen main set; 11/100 = 11% over all traced sessions), and were re-run with int8 KV at 64K like the main set. Of the 11, 10 submitted a patch. sympy-23413 hit the 250-step limit after 250 turns.
- **Walk design: 15.4× here** (9.42× search, 9.61× first held-out). 14.1× without sympy-23413. **5-month design: 12.1×** (11.1× without sympy-23413).
- These sessions spend less time in tools, so the tools-only bound is 54× vs 17×, and the speedup is higher. The speedup tracks the tool share, as the model says it must. The lowest session (scikit-learn-25973, 11×) is the most tool-heavy.
- Report it as: the design generalizes to unseen tasks, and its gain grows with how LLM-bound the session is. Don't pool it into the 9.4× headline, since its task mix differs from the stratified sample.

## GPU past the wall (G2, `gpu/g2_sweep.sh`)

With fp16 KV kept in host memory (`-nkvo 1`), the 3080 decodes at 59 ms/token at 4K, 373 ms at 32K and 1.07 s at 96K (Q8_0; Q4_K_M within 3%). Past the wall it is **26× slower** than in VRAM at the same depth: the wall is a cliff, not a slope.

## Area (`experiments/area_sweep.py`, `figs/fig_area.py`)

Where the area goes, from ISAAC Table I: the arrays are under 1% of a tile; ADCs are 31% and the eDRAM buffer plus its bus 47%. In the headline design, 55% of the arrays hold KV sized for the longest (64K) session.

Three sourced area knobs:
- a lean tile without the eDRAM buffer and bus (2 months, assumption);
- 8, 4 or 2 ADCs per IMA (reads 1×, 2× or 4× longer);
- 4-bit cells (half the arrays, 1.27× area per array, 1 µs write-verify writes, 3 months).

Exact front over the crossbar subspace (3,456 designs):

| Area | Speedup | What it takes |
|---|---|---|
| 9,729 mm² | 9.42× | headline (ISAAC tile) |
| 5,833 mm² | 9.47× | lean tile |
| 3,692 mm² | 9.00× | + 4-bit cells |
| 2,872 mm² | 7.19× | + 4 ADCs per IMA |
| 2,461 mm² | 5.13× | + 2 ADCs per IMA |
| 1,613 mm² | 1.78× | + no transposed K writes |
| < 1,600 mm² | — | a 64K context does not fit in crossbars |

Under each area cap (10 caps, full space, budget 150):
- **Walk:** within 0.6% of the exact front at every cap, in 31–90 evaluations.
- **TPE:** matches it only uncapped and at 6,000 mm² (there it leaves the subspace and reaches 9.48×). At 8,000 mm² it returns 9.01× (9.47× available); below 6,000 it returns 7.79× (5,000 mm² cap), 0.63× (4,000), 1.97× (3,000), and 0.63× (2,500 and 2,000), against 9.00, 9.00, 7.19, 5.13 and 1.78× available.
- **Walk fix needed:** the first version of the walk failed under caps (0.04×). It rejected every speed move that broke the cap. The fix reads the area term like any other binding term: a faster move that breaks the cap is paid for with the area move that costs least time (lean tile, 4-bit cells, fewer ADCs, fewer spare arrays, int8 KV). The moves it pays with are logged with the step.

Sensitivity to the derived 4-bit-cell area factor (1.27×). This is the walk's best design under each cap:

| 4-b area factor | cap 4,000 mm² | cap 3,000 mm² | cap 2,500 mm² |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0× | 8.95× | 8.95× | 7.16× |
| **1.27× (used)** | **8.95×** | **7.16×** | **5.12×** |
| 1.5× | 7.16× | 5.12× | 1.97× |
| 2.0× (4-b cells no longer pay off) | 6.54× | 4.79× | 1.34× |

The area front shifts by one step per +25% in that factor. **It is the number to source before quoting the area front.** A measured 4-bit-per-cell ReRAM CIM macro (ISSCC/VLSI 22 nm) is the right reference.

How to present it: a GPU cannot buy single-session latency with area, because decode at batch 1 is bound by memory bandwidth and a second GPU does not halve it. A crossbar coprocessor can, and the front is that trade. Iso-area with the 3080 die (628 mm²) is out of reach at ISAAC's 32 nm for a full 64K context. The 22 nm and 12 nm ReRAM silicon (ISSCC 2019–2024) is where the next factor comes from (future work, not modeled).

Same workload (45 search / 44 held-out sessions), same 17 knobs, same searchers, budget 100; walk once (deterministic), TPE and random over seeds 0–2. Only the harness inputs change. Output: `results/search/generality_main.json` (21 runs, ~20 min on 4 workers).

| Config | Walk | TPE (3 seeds) | Random (3 seeds) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **main**: `lib/tech`, measured host profile (reference, current code) | 9.42× at eval 28 (31 evals) | 9.46 / 9.42 / 8.53× at 100 / 88 / 64 | 4.71 / 2.45 / 7.43× |
| **dram**: crossbars removed; MVM role filled by DRAM-PIM (UPMEM or GDDR6-AiM) | 5.22× at eval 23 (29 evals), AiM | 5.24 / 5.23 / 5.24× at 93 / 80 / 65, AiM | 3.07 / 1.25 / 3.89×, AiM |
| **host_roof**: `lib/tech`, host = `cpu_roofline` (datasheet roofline, no measured profile) | 9.42× at eval 22 (25 evals) | 9.48 / 9.01 / 9.46× at 73 / 61 / 78 | 4.95 / 2.45 / 7.43× |

Held-out speedups track the search set in every run (within ±0.2×). Walk trails are in the JSON.

What this supports:
1. **The harness runs unchanged on a second library.** With the crossbars removed, the walk reads the same way: transfer binds (69%) → softmax and norms off the host; host binds → attention to the MVM tech; `upmem_dpu` binds (93%) → more DPUs (2,560 → 4,096), which reaches only 0.11×; still `upmem_dpu` binds → the other MVM tech (AiM), 0.85×; then LM head, nonlinear and vector-unit sizing up to 5.22×. It stops with `aim_gddr6` binding at 86% of LLM time and no move left (the unit-count knob is at its top). It lands within 0.4% of TPE's best in a third of the evaluations; random search reaches 1.3–3.9×.
2. **UPMEM is rejected, as expected** (`lib/tech/pim.yaml` says so): with its measured transfer law (1.82 ms + 1.68 µs × N per offload) and 96 MOPS per DPU it never passes 0.11× of the 3080, and the readings say why (DPU compute, then transfer).
3. **A second host profile changes the path, not the answer.** On the roofline host the host-bound start design is 0.12× instead of 0.04× (the datasheet roofline is about 3× faster than the measured host on the ops left there), the walk takes 8 speed moves instead of 12 (it never routes norms through the vector unit) and lands on the same design (9.42×, 9,729 mm², 7 months) in 25 evaluations instead of 31. TPE needs 61–100 evaluations and one seed stops at 9.01×.

Caveats (state them if E8 goes in the paper):
- **The DRAM-PIM numbers are mostly not checked.** UPMEM (`lib/tech/pim.yaml`) is measured silicon (VIPER case study: 48 MOPS int8 MAC/DPU, transfer law), but its MRAM bank bandwidth is marked VERIFY. Every AiM number (`lib/tech_dram/aim.yaml`: 1 TFLOPS and 1 TB/s internal per 1 GB chip, 100 ns per-op overhead) is recalled from Kwon et al., ISSCC 2022, and marked VERIFY; the per-op overhead is our assumption.
- AiM computes in BF16, but the harness charges weights and KV at int8/fp16 (the design's precision), which flatters AiM's traffic by up to 2×.
- DRAM-PIM area is 0 by convention (memory dies, not logic), so area and the shrink phase are not meaningful for the dram config; compare speed only.
- DRAM-PIM sizing is a new assumption: `xbar_scale` sizes the MVM tech relative to max(units its memory needs, `units_ref`), with `units_ref` 2,048 DPUs (so 1.25× = the measured 2,560-DPU server) and 8 AiM chips. The dram optimum (5.2×, 20 chips) sits at the top of the knob range; a wider range would go further, so 5.2× is not a bound for DRAM-PIM.
- The UPMEM transfer law is charged on transfers into the DPUs only (as in the integrator's VIPER form), not on the gather back.
- The roofline host is a datasheet-level profile of the same i9-12900K, not a second machine; "second host" here means a second host *model*.
- The "main" row is a rerun with the current 17-knob code. The `search_compare.py --tag main` numbers in the table above predate the three area knobs; walk and its final design match, the baselines differ (TPE 8.53–9.46× here vs 9.46–9.48× there, random seed 0 4.71× vs 8.15×).

Code changes this needed (main-library results unchanged; walk and random seed 0 reproduce bit-for-bit against the previous commit): `Space` maps the `xbar_tech` knob to any library tech (`mvm_techs`) and sizes non-crossbar MVM techs as above; the evaluator dedupes through `Space.key` (crossbar area knobs are inert for DRAM-PIM); the walk gets a generic rule for a binding unit tech it has no specific rule for (more units, or the other MVM tech); a tech may carry its own transfer law (`xfer_t0_s`, `xfer_s_per_unit`).

## Roofline vs Domeline (E1) (`experiments/roofline_vs_domeline.py`, `rvd_analyze.py`, `figs/fig_rvd_teaser.py`)

**Scope.** The 280,296 feasible designs of the 575,400-design sweep, i.e. the 14-knob space: the three area knobs stay at their defaults (ISAAC tile, 8 ADCs/IMA, 2-b cells). Feasible means no design crosses the capacity wall inside the workload (64K), so the wall shows up only in the 128K context point and in E2.

**Roofline definition** (plan item 3; `experiments/roofline.py`). Per op, max(flops/peak, bytes/BW) on the op's device, summed per step and replayed over the same sessions as the Domeline (exactly the same grid, tokens and contexts; checked against `WorkloadReplayer` to 4 digits). No link, NoC, launch, writes (KV appends and reprogramming dropped) or wall. Variants: **main** leaves host ops out; **host** puts them on the host roof (200 GOPS, measured STREAM 57 GB/s). The crossbar roof is the device peak (all arrays at once); a per-op crossbar roof (only the arrays holding that matrix, **local**) is reported as a check. Common vocabulary: crossbar, vector unit (vfu), softmax unit (star), host, transfer, launch, write, capacity; Roofline can only ever name the first four.

**Context grid, all 280,296 feasible designs** (single decode steps; 14 min on 20 cores; `results/search/rvd_ctx_main.json`). Host variant:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| decode 4K | 98.3% | 19.7% | 0.24 | 0.998 | 1–913, 1.00–1.60× | 0.1 / 0.52 / 0.57 |
| decode 16K | 98.4% | 52.3% | 0.41 | 0.980 | 1–625, 1.00–1.51× | 0.1 / 0.52 / 0.78 |
| decode 32K | 98.3% | 42.7% | 0.28 | 0.962 | 1–617, 1.00–1.42× | 0.1 / 0.52 / 0.75 |

- **Plainly: over the whole feasible space, the Roofline mostly agrees with the Domeline.** With host ops on the host roof it names the Domeline's binding term for 94–99% of designs at every context inside the workload and ranks designs with Spearman 0.96–0.998. The reason is the space: most feasible designs are bound by the vector unit doing attention (57–75%) or by the host (24–42%), and both are textbook roofline costs (the vfu is compute-bound at 4 GOPS per unit; the Domeline adds only a 5 ns op overhead). This triggers the fallback (plan item 10).
- **Main variant (host ops left out) is degenerate**: designs that keep everything on the host cost 0, so its "best" design is 235–1,700× slower than the optimum under the Domeline, and it agrees only 57–76% (but 98–100% where the Domeline's binding term is a coprocessor device).
- **Where it disagrees is where the good designs are.** Among the Domeline's top 1%, the Roofline names the wrong bottleneck for 33–80% of designs (it says "softmax unit compute" or "vector unit"; the Domeline says crossbar reads, or transfers / the wall), and its time is 2.5–4× too optimistic (median). The per-op crossbar roof (local) fixes the bottleneck name in the top 1% at 4K–32K (76–82%) and gets decode time within 0.7–1.1× there, but is 2.5× pessimistic on prefill (no layer pipelining) and names the crossbar for every top-1% prefill design, where the Domeline says softmax unit, vfu or transfers (0% agreement).
- **Roofline cannot separate the designs that matter.** Its best is an 18-way tie at every context: xbar_scale 2.0 (the device peak grows with arrays, so it prefers the biggest crossbar), int16 KV (more arrays again), with either crossbar tech and any link. At session level (sweep numbers) these are 8.05–9.48×: blind to writes, it cannot tell the transposable crossbar (9.48×) from ISAAC's (8.05×, K appends write 128 rows). And its picks are 30,073 mm² (25,930 mm² for ISAAC) where the Domeline's pick does 9.42–9.47× at 9,729 mm²: 3.1× the area for the same speed.
- **Past the wall** (128K, beyond the workload, where 28.9% of feasible designs run out of room): agreement drops to 70% overall and to 33% in the Domeline's top 10%, where the binding term is the wall (reprogramming writes) for 66% of designs. The Roofline sees none of them.

- **The good designs are exactly where the Roofline is blind.** The Domeline's top 100 designs are all crossbar-bound; the device-peak Roofline names the softmax unit for all 100. Over all 1,152 crossbar-bound designs (6.5-9.5x vs the 3080, i.e. every design near the optimum), the Roofline names the crossbar for none (vector unit 624, softmax unit 528): with the device peak as the roof its crossbar time is 347x too small (median), because a crossbar's cost is read latency per array activation, not throughput over all arrays. The per-op roof (local) recovers part of it (69% agreement in the top 1%) but is then 1.3x pessimistic.
- Elsewhere the Roofline agrees: 93% of all designs, Spearman 0.978, because most of the space is bound by the vector unit or the host, which are roofline costs. Among designs that beat the GPU (10,704), agreement is 81%.
- Its best design is an 18-way tie that it cannot break, spanning 8.05-9.48x in the Domeline (rank 1-457): it cannot see writes (ISAAC vs transposable crossbar), the link or area.

**E2 candidate (Fig. 1 draft, `figures/rvd_teaser.pdf`).** The headline design with its crossbar at 0.75× of what a 64K context needs (25% less area: 7,297 vs 9,729 mm²):
- Roofline: bound by the softmax unit's compute; 0.034 ms/token at 16K and 0.10 ms at 64K; session 15.5× vs the 3080 (LLM time 17.2 s), *the same as the full-size design* (15.5×). It would pick the smaller design.
- Domeline: bound by crossbar reads (0.35 ms floor) below the wall; the KV cache outgrows the arrays at 35,457 tokens, inside the workload (the 64K session), and reprogramming writes then bind: 4.2 ms/token at 64K, 41× the Roofline's estimate. Session 8.46× (LLM time 220 s) vs 9.42× for the full-size design. The design is infeasible: it does not hold the workload's longest session.
