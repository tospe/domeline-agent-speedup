# Results so far (Oct 22, 2026)

Every number below is regenerated by the scripts named next to it. The PUM side is model-only (the integrator, VIPER's closed form generalized), with technology numbers from `lib/tech` (several marked VERIFY). The GPU side is measured on this host's RTX 3080.

## Workload (T, `figs/fig_trace_profile.py`)

- 89 SWE-bench Verified sessions: mini-swe-agent 1.17.5 with mini-coder-4b (Q8_0) on the 3080, a repo-stratified sample. 82 submitted a patch.
- Time on the 3080 splits into **decode 90%, prefill 4%, tools 6%**. Tool time is net of the ~0.28 s container exec overhead per call; raw times are in `results/traces/swebench_summary.tsv`.
- Final context: median 15K tokens, max 64K. **8% of sessions (7/89) ran out of VRAM with fp16 KV** (wall at 32–36K) and had to be re-run with int8 KV at 64K. One of them (xarray-4094, 218 turns) outgrew even 64K.
- Cross-check (G3): replaying each trace through the llama-bench decode curve predicts the measured decode time within −2 to −3%.

## GPU wall (G1, `figs/fig_gpu_wall.py`)

| Weights | KV fp16 | KV int8 | KV int4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q8_0 | 32–36K | 64–80K | 96–128K |
| Q4_K_M | 40–48K | 80–96K | 128–160K |

Q8_0 decode: 7.5 ms/token at an empty context, 14 ms at 32K (fp16 KV). Int8 KV is slower at the same depth.

## Headline design (`experiments/search_compare.py --tag main`)

The Domeline walk finds it in 28 evaluations (45 search sessions):

- Crossbars (ISAAC geometry, transposable K writes) for all weight MVMs, the LM head and attention; 64 fixed-function nonlinear units for softmax, norms, RoPE and SiLU; embedding and sampling on the CPU; CXL-class link; int8 KV. 2.16M arrays.
- **9.42× faster than the RTX 3080 on the search sessions, 9.61× on the 44 held-out sessions.** The Amdahl bound (tools only) is 16.7×.
- Decode is 0.40 ms/token at 4K context and 0.77 ms/token at 64K (3080: 7.5–28 ms). The crossbar read cycle (348 µs/token) is the floor. At long context, score traffic between the crossbars and the nonlinear unit is the next term (transfer: 28% of LLM time).
- Area: **9,729 mm² at ISAAC's 32 nm tile density, ~15× the 3080's die.** Effort: 7 months.

## The walk (`figs/fig_walk.py main`)

0.04× (crossbar MVMs, everything else on the host) → attention to crossbars 0.45× → softmax to nonlinear unit 0.80× → LM head to crossbars 1.39× → norms off the host 1.75× → unit sizing 2.5–6.5× → transposed K writes 7.4× → norms to nonlinear unit 8.5× → 9.42×. Then shrink: 18.8K → 9.7K mm² at the same speed. Every move cites the reading that justified it.

## Search efficiency (`figs/fig_search.py`)

Budget 100 evaluations on the search set; "best" = the smallest design within 1% of the fastest found.

| | Unconstrained | Effort ≤ 5 months |
|---|---|---|
| Domeline walk | 9.42× at eval 28 | 8.00× at eval 33 |
| TPE (3 seeds) | 9.46–9.48× at 77–99 | 3.27 / 7.02 / 8.05× |
| Hill climbing, same moves, time only (3 seeds) | 9.47 / 9.47 / 3.26× | 3.08 / 3.26 / 8.01× |
| Random (3 seeds) | 3.1–8.2× | 0.84–3.06× |
| Sonnet agent + readings | 9.47× at eval 14 | 8.00–8.01× at 11–13 |
| Sonnet agent, time only | 9.47× at eval 6 | 8.00–8.05× at 12–15 |
| Haiku agent + readings (3 seeds) | — | 8.05 / 8.05 / 7.02× |
| Haiku agent, time only (3 seeds) | — | 8.05 / 8.05 / 8.00× |

What this supports:
1. The readings make algorithmic search reliable: the walk always finds the best design in about 30 evaluations; hill climbing over the same single-knob moves, without readings, finds it on 1–2 of 3 seeds.
2. Classical searchers need 3× more evaluations (TPE) or fail (random).
3. **LLM agents do not need the readings in this space.** Their hardware priors, plus the knob descriptions, find the optimum in 6–15 evaluations. Don't claim the readings help LLM agents.
4. At equal speed the walk's design costs less effort (7 vs 10–16 months), because the shrink phase drops the on-die link that only buys the last 0.5%.

## Effort staircase (`experiments/effort_sweep.py`, `figs/fig_effort.py`)

0 months 0.59× (crossbars only) → 1 month 3.07× (vector unit) → 4 months 8.00× (nonlinear unit) → 7 months 9.42× (transposed K writes). TPE finds nothing feasible below 4 months in 150 evaluations.

## Sensitivity (`experiments/sensitivity.py`, `figs/fig_sensitivity.py`)

Base 9.52× on all 89 sessions. Each uncertain number is scaled by ½ and 2, with the walk re-optimizing. Only the **crossbar read cycle** moves the headline much (11.0× / 7.5×). Nonlinear-unit throughput and bandwidth move it by ≤4%. Link, crossbar write latency, vector-unit throughput and the host attention kernel move it by <1%. **Per-array crossbar area moves area linearly (4.9K–19.5K mm²) and leaves speed unchanged.** The VERIFY entries that matter are the read cycle and the per-array area.

## Exhaustive sweep (`experiments/sweep_all.py`, `sweep_analyze.py`, `figs/fig_sweep.py`)

All 575,400 distinct designs on the 45 search sessions (11.5 h on 20 cores). 280,296 are feasible.

- **The optimum under the model is 9.48×.** The walk's design (9.42×, 9,729 mm², 7 months) is the smallest design within 1% of it, which is the rule every searcher's "best" uses.
- **Good designs are rare.** Only 3.8% of feasible designs beat the 3080. 96 designs (0.03%) are within 1% of the optimum, and 144 are within 10%. Random search's failure follows directly from this.
- **The effort staircase is exact.** The best design under each effort cap in the sweep matches what the walk found: 0.59 / 3.08 / 8.00 / 9.42×.
- **How close each searcher got, unconstrained (gap to 9.48×, eval of first design within 1%):**
  - walk: 0.7% (the smallest-design rule), at eval 20;
  - TPE: 0.07–0.29%, at evals 41–69;
  - hill climbing: 0.17% on 2 of 3 seeds, at evals 56–78; 66% on the third;
  - random: 14–68%, never;
  - Sonnet agent: 0.18%, at eval 10 with readings and eval 4 without.
- **Under the 5-month cap (optimum 8.05×):**
  - walk: 0.56%, at eval 24;
  - TPE: within 1% on 1 of 3 seeds;
  - hill climbing: within 1% on 1 of 3 seeds;
  - Sonnet and Haiku agents: within 1% in 9–15 evals on 9 of 10 runs.
- Exhaustive search needs 575,400 evaluations to find the optimum. The walk needs 20–30, so this space did not need brute force. The model makes brute force affordable here, but not with a gem5-class evaluator at 1–3 h per point.

## Second held-out set (`experiments/heldout_extra.py`)

These are 11 SWE-bench tasks that Docker Hub rate limits blocked during collection. They were run after every search had finished, so no search, library or space decision saw them. The design is built exactly as in the search.

- 4 of 11 hit the fp16 wall (36%, vs 8% in the main set), and were re-run with int8 KV at 64K like the main set. Of the 11, 10 submitted a patch. sympy-23413 hit the 250-step limit after 250 turns.
- **Walk design: 15.4× here** (9.42× search, 9.61× first held-out). 14.1× without sympy-23413. **5-month design: 12.1×** (11.1× without sympy-23413).
- These sessions spend less time in tools, so the tools-only bound is 54× vs 17×, and the speedup is higher. The speedup tracks the tool share, as the model says it must. The lowest session (scikit-learn-25973, 11×) is the most tool-heavy.
- Report it as: the design generalizes to unseen tasks, and its gain grows with how LLM-bound the session is. Don't pool it into the 9.4× headline, since its task mix differs from the stratified sample.

## GPU past the wall (G2, `gpu/g2_sweep.sh`)

With fp16 KV kept in host memory (`-nkvo 1`), the 3080 decodes at 59 ms/token at 4K, 373 ms at 32K and 1.07 s at 96K (Q8_0; Q4_K_M within 3%). Past the wall it is **26× slower** than in VRAM at the same depth: the wall is a cliff, not a slope.

## Area (`experiments/area_sweep.py`, `figs/fig_area.py`)

Where the area goes, from ISAAC Table I: the arrays are under 1% of a tile; ADCs are 31% and the eDRAM buffer plus its bus 47%. In the headline design, 55% of the arrays hold KV sized for the longest (64K) session.

Three sourced area knobs:
- a lean tile without the eDRAM buffer and bus (2 months, assumption);
- 8, 4 or 2 ADCs per IMA (reads 1×, 2× or 4× longer);
- 4-bit cells (half the arrays, 1.27× area per array, 1 µs write-verify writes, 3 months).

Exact front over the crossbar subspace (3,456 designs):

| Area | Speedup | What it takes |
|---|---|---|
| 9,729 mm² | 9.42× | headline (ISAAC tile) |
| 5,833 mm² | 9.47× | lean tile |
| 3,692 mm² | 9.00× | + 4-bit cells |
| 2,872 mm² | 7.19× | + 4 ADCs per IMA |
| 2,461 mm² | 5.13× | + 2 ADCs per IMA |
| 1,613 mm² | 1.78× | + no transposed K writes |
| < 1,600 mm² | — | a 64K context does not fit in crossbars |

Under each area cap (10 caps, full space, budget 150):
- **Walk:** within 0.6% of the exact front at every cap, in 31–90 evaluations.
- **TPE:** matches it only at caps ≥ 6,000 mm². Below that it returns 7.79× (5,000 mm² cap), 0.63× (4,000), 1.97× (3,000), and 0.63× (2,500 and 2,000), against 9.00, 9.00, 7.19, 5.13 and 1.78× available.
- **Walk fix needed:** the first version of the walk failed under caps (0.04×). It rejected every speed move that broke the cap. The fix reads the area term like any other binding term: a faster move that breaks the cap is paid for with the area move that costs least time (lean tile, 4-bit cells, fewer ADCs, fewer spare arrays, int8 KV). The moves it pays with are logged with the step.

How to present it: a GPU cannot buy single-session latency with area, because decode at batch 1 is bound by memory bandwidth and a second GPU does not halve it. A crossbar coprocessor can, and the front is that trade. Iso-area with the 3080 die (628 mm²) is out of reach at ISAAC's 32 nm for a full 64K context. The 22 nm and 12 nm ReRAM silicon (ISSCC 2019–2024) is where the next factor comes from (future work, not modeled).
